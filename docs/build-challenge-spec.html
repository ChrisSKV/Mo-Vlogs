What the research changed

Twenty-two agents spent three million tokens on Mo Vlogs, Base44, opt-in conversion data, competitor teardowns, contest mechanics and compliance. Five findings move the plan. The first three were not in the brief at all.

FINDING 01 · CHANGES THE STRATEGY

Mo already has two live lead funnels

movlogscircle.com is live and is line one of every video description since 8 August. It collects name, email and phone behind an SMS one-time-code. Headline: "A private club for ambitious people." No price, no product, no dates — a waitlist. winwithmo.com is a second funnel: an iPhone giveaway collecting name, email and WhatsApp, already bilingual English/Arabic.

So the Build Challenge is not launching into empty space. It is a third funnel competing for the same finite traffic, and the Circle currently owns the only link slot in his descriptions.

What we do: build the Build Challenge as the acquisition engine and treat the Circle as where winners and near-misses land. But the client has to decide which URL goes in the description on drop day — they cannot both be line one.

FINDING 02 · SECURITY — TELL THE CLIENT TODAY

Their internal ops runbook is publicly readable

movlogscircle.com/SECURITY-OTP.md returns HTTP 200 to anyone. It names their Supabase project reference, documents two SMS-pumping attacks against them (around 4,500 sends in one hour on 14 August; around 1,500 more on 22 August), lists every rate-limit value and its default, and flags a legacy lead edge function that "inserts leads with NO Turnstile/caps/checks" and is still deployed.

What we do: flag it to Dzigi immediately — this is their existing property, not ours. And make sure no equivalent file ships in our build.

FINDING 03 · ANSWERS YOUR ARABIC QUESTION

The 8.92M Arabic channel is not the audience you think

ARABIC MO VLOGS is not dubbed vlogs. It is MrBeast-style challenge content — "Squid Game $50,000", "Last person to leave the bed wins $10,000" — 159 videos, biggest Short at 132M views. That is a children-and-teens Shorts audience with close to zero purchase intent, and a real safeguarding problem for a funnel that dials phone numbers.

But Arabic is still right, for a different reason. winwithmo.com's country dial-code list is hand-ordered, and the order is their own audience model written down: UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman, then the Levant and Egypt, then the West, then South Asia. Their existing code also sets OTP_HOME_PREFIX=971 and exempts the UAE from every rate limit.

Answer for Dzigi: yes to Arabic — for the Gulf commercial audience, not for the 8.92M challenge channel. And do not promote this on the challenge channel at all.

FINDING 04 · CHANGES THE POSITIONING

His audience has voted against "teach me" twice

Mo Crypto: 41,500 subscribers and 1.25M lifetime views across 90 videos — about 13,800 a video. His one make-money video on the main channel, a dropshipping tutorial, did 93,000 against a 300,000–800,000 baseline for neighbouring uploads. A roughly fourfold underperformance.

Worse, there is a 1.16M-view Atozy exposé documenting Mo promoting UpStake, a token called a Ponzi on bitcointalk in March 2019. And scammers have actively impersonated him to harvest personal details — he posted publicly to warn fans about it.

What we do: lead with the competition and the money, never with "we'll teach you". Rule out the entire vocabulary of passive income, ROI, trading and "make $X". And treat anti-impersonation proof as conversion copy, because a dark page with a gold logo asking for name, email and phone is the exact silhouette of the scams his fans were warned about.

FINDING 05 · CHANGES THE FORECAST AND THE BUILD

Two ceilings nobody had measured

Reach. His last four longform uploads did 280k, 374k, 330k and 445k views. A year ago the same slots did 1.7M to 3.1M. Like rates run 0.9–2.1% against a healthy 3–5%. Forecast from 300–450k a video, not from 11.9M subscribers.

Delivery. SMS sender-ID registration is a multi-week regulatory process in every one of the top markets — TDRA in the UAE, CITC in Saudi with Arabic pre-approval, DLT in India, 10DLC in the US. WhatsApp Business template approval plus number warming is the same. So "we'll text you to confirm" is not a 48-hour capability.

What we do: email confirmation is the day-one channel. SMS and WhatsApp go behind a feature flag that flips without a redeploy once registration clears. The thank-you page renders the email variant until then.

01

The offer and the number

Amount — my call Terms — needs Mo Ring-fencing — blocks the strong headline

You asked me to pick a number. It is $50,000, structured as an equity investment on published standard terms — not a cash prize.

Why $50,000 and not $1,000,000

The number has to clear two bars at once: big enough to make someone stop scrolling and type their real phone number, small enough that Mo will actually ring-fence it. Those bars fight each other, and most creators resolve the fight by picking a huge number they never have to pay.

A million dollars reads as MrBeast money. Mo's channel does not carry MrBeast's proof history — it carries an Atozy video with 1.16M views. On his page, a million-dollar promise is not exciting, it is the tell. Fifty thousand is a number a successful Dubai creator plausibly writes personally, which means the audience believes it, which is the only thing that matters.

It is also genuinely life-changing across the actual audience geography. For a builder in Egypt, Pakistan or India, $50,000 is real seed capital. And it is small enough to sit in a separate account with a screenshot — which unlocks the strongest sentence available to us.

Why an investment and not a giveaway

Three reasons, and they all point the same way. Dubai DET generally does not permit cash prizes in sales promotions, so equity is the safer structure. "I invest in your business" is what the brief actually said. And an investment is what a real investor does — it is more credible than a cheque, not less, because it means Mo is taking the same risk the entrant is.

The model to copy is a16z Speedrun: one standard deal, published in full, identical for everyone, no negotiation. $50,000 for 10% on a standard SAFE implies a $500,000 post-money valuation, which is a defensible pre-seed number for a six-week-old product. Publish the deal document as a PDF before entries open.

The decision that changes the headline

If the $50,000 is genuinely ring-fenced in a separate account, we can write "Mo invests $50,000" and "the money is already set aside". If it stays discretionary, both lines become misleading claims sitting next to terms that reserve discretion, and we have to drop to "Compete for Mo's investment". That is a materially weaker page. Ring-fencing is the single highest-return thing the client can do this week, and it costs them nothing but a transfer between their own accounts.

The ladder

A single-winner offer depresses conversion, because 99.9% of readers correctly calculate they will not win and stop reading. Bolt's $1M hackathon paid first through tenth plus regional and category prizes and pulled over 100,000 participants. Every reader needs to find a rung they believe they can reach.

Two corrections the compliance pass forced. "Flown to Dubai" is undeliverable for a large share of this audience — visa refusal is the normal case for several top source countries — so it needs an explicit alternative for anyone who cannot travel. And we cannot grant Base44 Elite licences; that is Wix's product to give, not ours, unless it is secured in writing.

The named categories also need published criteria. "Most Beautiful App" has none, and the entire legal defence of this thing rests on every award being made against pre-published objective criteria — that is what makes it a skill contest rather than a lottery.

Why "application" beats "opt-in"

Mechanically identical — same three fields, same button, same friction. But it buys three things a course opt-in cannot have. A reason for the call that isn't a pitch: "I'm looking at your entry." A reason the phone field is honest, because an application needs a way to reach you. And a lawful structure, because a competition with a published rubric and free entry is a skill contest, while the same thing with a purchase requirement is a lottery.

The answer rate on the first dial is the metric that decides whether this account makes money. Application framing is the cheapest lever we have on it.

Total to the bottom edge of the button: 626px. That leaves 14px of headroom. There is none to give away.

The photo is the line everyone will push on. 150px is the ceiling — every extra 20px of Mo pushes the button out of the fold. It is also the LCP element, so it ships as AVIF with a WebP fallback at 24–40KB, priority, cropped 375×150 on his face.

What is not up there

No video — the client ruled it out and the budget agrees. No logo lockup. No countdown timer anywhere on the page. No supercars, no watch, no mansion: his real thumbnails carry almost no text overlay, and a posed luxury portrait raises persuasion knowledge and lowers intent. Plain photo, plain page.

The microcopy is the highest-leverage block on the page

Michael Aagaard's well-known test cut a form from nine fields to six and lost 14%; he restored all nine, rewrote the per-field explainer copy, and gained 19%. Explanation beats deletion.

Those three sentences do four jobs at once. They reframe the phone number from a toll into a delivery address. They name a real deliverable. They disclose that a paid programme exists before consent is given — which is the difference between a closer who has a conversation and a closer who has an argument. And they pre-frame the call so the dial is expected rather than an ambush.

The Build Pack has to be real

The button says we send it and the microcopy says we send it. If it does not exist, both are false and the whole honesty argument on the page collapses at the first delivery. It is a launch blocker with no owner in any version of this brief so far. It does not need to be elaborate — a short PDF, the four app ideas that actually work on Base44, the prompt structure, what to check before you show anyone. But it needs a name against it today.

What is deliberately not on this form

  • No one-time code. Their existing funnel caps at 150 SMS an hour globally, and 8 an hour per non-UAE country prefix, and it fails closed. A video drop blows through that in minutes and silently kills conversion for everyone outside the Gulf.
  • No age field. It costs opt-in rate at the exact moment of decision. Age moves to the thank-you page, where it becomes a required blocking step and costs us nothing — the lead is already banked.
  • No last name, no country dropdown, no "tell us about your idea". All of it moves downstream.

Engineering notes

  • Fail open. Bank the lead in Neon before any qualification and return 200 even when the write fails. A lead that reached us slowly beats a visitor who bounced on an error.
  • Consent is geo-conditional, resolved server-side from /api/geo at render time. The US, India and the Gulf need materially different wording.
  • crypto.randomUUID() throws on this exact audience — it is unavailable in insecure contexts and on Android WebView below 92. Old Androids in South Asia and MENA are precisely our traffic, and the ID generation sits in the critical path, so this is a silent total form failure for those users. Ship a fallback and set an explicit legacy browserslist target.
  • keepalive: true on the submit POST. We redirect optimistically while the write is in flight; without it the navigation cancels the request on slow connections.
  • Reuse phone-input.tsx and lead.ts from Kingsplay wholesale. Both are already handling international E.164 validation and the session handoff.
05

Page 2 — the thank you

You had this as "keep your phone close". It is worth much more than that. This is the page that decides whether the phone gets answered, and answer rate is the number the whole account lives on.

In order

  1. Confirmation and the number. "We'll call the number ending in 47" — we already have last2() for this, so we confirm the number without reprinting it.
  2. The age gate. Required, blocking, step 1 of 2. Not an optional chip. His audience genuinely skews young and the floor cannot be dialling minors — and we cannot take equity from one either.
  3. The micro-commitment. Step 2 of 2, and the single most valuable element on the page: one open text field, "What do you want to build?" That sentence becomes the closer's opener — "I'm looking at your entry about the app for your dad's shop" — and it is the highest-signal data in the entire funnel.
  4. Four one-tap questions, ungated. Nothing blocks, everything sorts.
  5. When to call you — hand-typed, not a dropdown. People write "after 6, I'm at work" and that is worth more than a time slot.
  6. What happens next — three steps, spelled out.
  7. Start tonight — kills the dead time before the call. Link them to Base44's free tier and the Build Pack.

Speed to lead is the whole game

The classic lead-response research is unambiguous: contact rates collapse by an order of magnitude between a five-minute callback and a thirty-minute one. Every element on this page exists to buy the floor those first five minutes — the number confirmation, the expectation setting, the "it'll be an unknown number" warning. None of it works if the floor isn't actually staffed when the video drops.

What is explicitly not on this page: no upsell, no price, no checkout, no countdown. They have already converted. The only job left is making the phone get answered.

06

The rules page

We produce itClient reviews

You told Dzigi we would handle this. It is not a formality — the rules page is what converts a possible illegal lottery into a lawful skill contest, and publishing it well is a trust asset rather than a liability.

The legal test is simple: prize plus chance plus consideration equals lottery. We remove chance with a published rubric and named judges. We remove consideration with genuinely free entry. Both have to be real, not decorative.

What the page must contain

  • Promoter — legal entity name and Dubai address. Not "Mo Vlogs".
  • Eligibility — 18+, able to legally own and sign for a company, territories in and territories out.
  • Free entry, stated unconditionally — and that buying anything confers no advantage.
  • The rubric with its weights, identical to the landing page.
  • Judges, named, with real one-line bios.
  • Dates — entries close, shortlist published, final filmed, winner contacted within seven days.
  • What "investment" means — equity, a standard SAFE, subject to KYC and legal checks like any real investment. What happens if the winner cannot complete the paperwork: it goes to the runner-up.
  • Filming consent is required to win.
  • Entrants keep everything they build.
  • The graceful out, worded honestly — see below.
  • Not affiliated with Base44 or Wix. We use the product name to describe what we teach; we claim no endorsement, partnership or certification.

The tension, and how we resolve it

There is a real problem with black and gold here, and it should be said out loud: a dark page with a gold logo asking for name, email and phone is the exact silhouette of the scams his fans have been warned about. His own audience has been told to watch for people impersonating him to harvest details.

The resolution is not to abandon his brand. It is to spend the gold as an accent rather than as the substance, and to make the page unmistakably plain: a real photograph of Mo that looks like a photograph rather than a product shot, plain-spoken copy, the inline authenticity proof, and no ornamentation anywhere. Expensive-looking is the wrong target. Checkable is the right one.

There is a second tension worth naming for the client. His channel voice is loud — 78% of his titles end in "!!!", a third are all caps — and this page is quiet. Someone arriving from a "$100,000,000" thumbnail lands somewhere hushed. That contrast can be an asset, the reveal of the real Mo behind the spectacle, but only if the page acknowledges the transition. Otherwise it just reads as a different, unfamiliar brand.

The two directions we can decline into later

Not built now, noted so the client knows the options exist: a daylight editorial direction — white ground, his real photography, the money as the only colour, which maximally breaks the scam silhouette; and a channel-native direction that borrows the thumbnail register directly. I would test daylight against black-and-gold before I tested anything else on this page.

Qualification and the floor

Qualification is a sort order, not a gate. Nothing gets rejected at the door — leads get banked, scored, and dialled in priority order.

LayerWhereWhat it captures
1Page 1, invisibleGeo, device, traffic source, UTM, time on page, scroll depth. On 100% of leads.
2Page 1, below foldSelf-selection from the "who this is for / not for" block. Filters before the tap.
3Thank-you pageBlocking age gate, then four ungated one-tap chips.
4Thank-you pageThe open text field. Highest-signal data in the funnel.
5Server, pre-dialPhone validity, duplicate check, suppression list, local-time legality.
6The callWhere real qualification actually happens.

The capacity problem nobody has run the numbers on

A 300–450k-view upload at a 2–5% click rate produces roughly 6,000–22,000 sessions in 48 hours. At a 12–20% opt-in rate that is 1,000 to 4,000 leads, front-loaded into the first day. At six dial attempts each, that is 6,000 to 24,000 dial attempts.

Nobody has told us how many reps there are. Six reps at twelve dials an hour is about 576 dials a day. We need the headcount before launch, because if the floor can only work the top 400 leads, the other 3,600 need an email lane with a "call me now" button that promotes them into the queue — and that is a build item, not an afterthought.

The do-not-say list

Distribute to the floor before launch and QA-score recordings against it. No income claims of any kind. No "guaranteed". No suggestion that buying the training improves the odds of winning — that single sentence turns a lawful skill contest into an unlawful lottery, and it is the most likely thing an enthusiastic closer says unprompted.

09

Tracking

The existing Circle page carries no analytics of any kind — no GA, no GTM, no Meta pixel, no Clarity, nothing. There is no baseline to beat and no way to read the new funnel unless instrumentation ships with it.

  • Neon is the source of truth, not a pixel. First-party event log, server-side.
  • Microsoft Clarity from hour zero. Watching thirty real session recordings in the first hour of the drop will teach us more than any test we could run that day.
  • Error monitoring. A system designed to swallow every error and return 200 is blind by construction. Sentry goes in above the pixels.
  • Operational alerts to the team on opt-in rate collapse, form error spikes, and dial-queue depth.

No A/B test on launch day

100% on control for video one. A single drop is the best traffic this funnel will ever see and splitting it spends half of it on an arm we already suspect is worse. The first test runs on video two, when we know what normal looks like.

Questions for the client

Ordered by how much they block. The first four should go to Dzigi today.

Is the $50,000 ring-fenced, or discretionary?

Changes the headline, the prize block and the rules page. Ring-fenced unlocks "Mo invests $50,000" and "the money is already set aside" — the two strongest sentences available. Discretionary drops us to "Compete for Mo's investment", which is materially weaker. It costs them nothing but a transfer between their own accounts.

Which URL goes in the video description on drop day?

movlogscircle.com currently owns line one. The Build Challenge and the Circle cannot both be the primary link. If the Circle stays, we are getting secondary placement and the forecast halves.

How many reps, what hours, what timezones?

Decides whether we need the email lane and the "call me now" promotion mechanic, which is a real build item. A drop could produce 4,000 leads in a day against a floor that can work 500.

Do we exclude the UK and EEA?

My recommendation is yes. It is 2–4% of leads and it removes the single largest regulatory exposure in the document — DMCC with direct CMA fines at 10% of global turnover, CAP significant-conditions rules, GDPR representative, and TPS registration with a 28-day lead time we do not have. If the client says no, TPS registration has to start today.

Do we build the Arabic version, and who reviews it?

Yes, for the Gulf commercial audience — UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Kuwait. Not for the 8.92M challenge channel, which is a children's Shorts audience. Needs a native Gulf reviewer, not machine translation.

Who are the three judges, and have they agreed in writing?

Named judges with real bios are one of the documented markers separating legitimate competitions from prize scams. Announcing the panel is also its own content beat and its own email send.

Has anyone confirmed the DET promotion permit and the advertiser permit?

Sales promotions in Dubai generally need a DET permit, and creators have needed an advertiser permit since February 2026. Neither blocks the build; both block the drop.

Is there any prior investment Mo has made that we can show?

One line and a receipt about a business he has actually put money into, ideally with the owner on camera. "This isn't my first cheque" is the cheapest credibility in the entire funnel, and given the Atozy history it is worth real effort to find.
